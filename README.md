# Impulsive-Buying-Behavior-Research-Paper
Impact of Social Media Trends on Impulsive Buying Behavior: A Gender-Based Study
Objective:
- Examine whether social media trends and influencer engagement drive impulsive buying differently across genders among young consumers in Pune.

Dataset/Source:
- Primary data collected via a 23-question Google Forms survey (7 Likert-scale items) from 38 respondents (25 females, 13 males), predominantly aged 18–24, over a 1-week period.

Methods:
- Descriptive statistics (mean, variance)
- Independent samples T-tests (gender comparison on 7 Likert-scale questions)
- Hypothesis testing (α = 0.05)
- Visualizations: bar and pie charts for social media usage, influencer preference, and shopping behavior

Tools:
- Microsoft Excel (T-tests, descriptive stats, charts), Google Forms (data collection)

Key Findings
- No statistically significant gender difference in impulsive buying behavior (all p-values > 0.05)
- Both genders show planned/rational buying — comparing reviews and prices before purchase
- Males reported slightly higher social pressure to buy trending products (p = 0.066, closest to significance)
- Influencer type differs by gender: males favor celebrities, females favor macro-influencers
- Females spend more time on social media and follow more influencers, but this doesn't translate into higher impulsive buying
- Seasonal discounts are more effective purchase drivers than influencer promotions for both genders

Limitation: Small, gender-imbalanced sample (n=38) limits generalizability.
